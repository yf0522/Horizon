### [Tailcat: Netcat-like Utility Over Tailscale&\#x27;s Secure Data Plane](https://github.com/tailscale/tailcat) ⭐️ 8.0/10

Tailcat is a new open-source utility that extends netcat&\#x27;s functionality by operating over Tailscale&\#x27;s secure, peer-to-peer network. This tool simplifies direct and secure communication between devices, effectively solving common networking challenges such as NAT traversal. By combining netcat&\#x27;s simplicity with Tailscale&\#x27;s robust data plane, Tailcat offers a practical solution for developers and system administrators seeking secure, peer-to-peer connectivity.

hackernews · nderjung · Aug 26, 17:42 · [Discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49452990)

**「Background」** Netcat is a command-line utility for reading from and writing to network connections using TCP or UDP, often used for network debugging and data transfer. Tailscale builds secure, peer-to-peer networks using WireGuard, handling NAT traversal and relaying \(DERP\) through its data plane, while its control plane manages identity and access. Tailcat leverages Tailscale&\#x27;s open-source data plane components to provide netcat-like functionality, enabling direct, secure connections without requiring Tailscale&\#x27;s control plane, instead using a dedicated relay service for connection bootstrapping.

**「Impact」** Tailcat enables developers and users to establish secure, direct peer-to-peer connections between devices, simplifying communication across complex network environments like those requiring NAT traversal, as exemplified by its use as a transport for a Minecraft mod.

**「Community Discussion」** Community members highlighted a fun use case involving a Minecraft mod using Tailcat as its transport and questioned its similarity to other tools like Iroh. There was also discussion regarding Tailscale&\#x27;s use of Nix for development environments and a query about the underlying technical architecture, specifically how much of it remains &quot;Tailscale&quot; given WireGuard transport and a new control plane. Many expressed enthusiasm for the tool, noting its potential to enable innovation in trivial peer-to-peer communication as a strong alternative to ubiquitous IPv6.

3D printer manufacturer Bambu Lab is reportedly engaged in an ongoing violation of the AGPL license, raising significant concerns within the open-source community and hardware industry. This situation underscores the challenges of enforcing open-source licenses, particularly in the context of commercial hardware products. The community is actively discussing both technical workarounds for current owners and potential legal strategies to address the infringement.

hackernews · Velocifyer · Aug 26, 17:41 · [Discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49452980)

**「Background」** The GNU Affero General Public License \(AGPL\) is a free copyleft license, based on GPLv3, specifically designed for software run over a network, requiring that source code be made available when the software is accessed remotely, thereby closing the &quot;application service provider&quot; loophole. 3D printer manufacturer Bambu Lab has been accused by the Software Freedom Conservancy of violating the AGPLv3 license for its Bambu Studio software, a fork of PrusaSlicer, specifically concerning its proprietary \`libbambu\_networking\` library. Bambu Lab had previously faced accusations regarding this library and other components, leading to a partial backtrack from legal pressure against a developer. 

**「Impact」** Bambu Lab faces a formal investigation by the Software Freedom Conservancy for confirmed AGPLv3 violations, potentially leading to legal enforcement actions, and has already threatened a developer over related open-source code.

**「Community Discussion」** Community members have developed workarounds, such as using LAN mode with OrcaSlicer and the \`open-bamboo-networking\` plugin, to enable Bambu Lab printers to operate without connecting to the manufacturer&\#x27;s servers. There is a strong sentiment that enforcing AGPL against companies, particularly those in the Chinese tech industry, is challenging and may require legal action like blocking imports, though this demands significant financial resources. Some users, while acknowledging the license issues, appreciate the printers&\#x27; functionality and ease of use.

Alibaba Cloud&\#x27;s Tongyi has released Qwen3.8-Flash and open-sourced Qwen3.8-Flash-Next, a preview of the Qwen4 architecture, featuring a novel N-gram embedding architecture. Qwen3.8-Flash-Next comprises a 125B-parameter main model with an additional 51B N-gram embeddings, activating only 6B parameters per token, and boasts a native 262K context window extendable to 1M. hackernews · tosh · Aug 26, 12:52 · [Discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49448210)

**「Impact」** The reported efficiency, cost-effectiveness, and strong performance of Qwen3.8-Flash-Next in complex software engineering tasks could significantly lower the barrier for developers and organizations to integrate advanced AI capabilities into their development workflows.
