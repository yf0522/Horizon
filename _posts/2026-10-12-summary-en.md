An article critically examines passkeys, arguing they can be a &\#x27;trap&\#x27; due to significant concerns regarding portability, stringent attestation requirements, and limitations on user control over their security credentials. The analysis highlights potential pitfalls such as vendor lock-in and the complexities associated with managing these modern authentication methods. This critique provides valuable counterpoints and technical considerations for software engineers and security professionals evaluating passkey adoption.

hackernews · laurex · Oct 11, 21:59 · [Discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50048312)

**「Understanding Passkeys and Attestation」** Passkeys are a digital authentication method that replaces passwords by using a cryptographic key pair: a private key stored securely on the user&\#x27;s device and a public key registered with the website or application. An optional security feature called attestation allows a service to verify the legitimacy and provenance of the passkey being created, which can be enforced during the initial passkey registration process.

**「Impact」** The adoption of passkeys, intended to replace passwords and enhance security, presents users with potential challenges related to credential portability, vendor lock-in, and control over their authentication methods.

**「Community Discussion」** Community members voiced concerns about passkey portability, the inability to back up credentials, and the perceived weaker security of biometric unlocks compared to strong passwords. One commenter clarified that attestation is typically a registration-time requirement, not a login-time one, and browsers prompt users for permission. Notably, another commenter highlighted the article&\#x27;s own addendum, which suggests the author ultimately made a compelling case for passkeys by citing their domain-bound nature and resistance to Man-In-The-Middle attacks.

### [Sliced Wasserstein Embeddings and HNSW Enable Fast Color-Scheme Search for 48k Paintings](https://www.reddit.com/r/MachineLearning/comments/1x39uu6/sliced_wasserstein_embeddings_hnsw_for/) ⭐️ 8.0/10

Palette Atlas has developed an efficient system for searching 48,695 public-domain paintings by color scheme, addressing the slowness of Earth Mover&\#x27;s Distance \(EMD\) which takes about 6 ms per pair. The solution employs Sliced Wasserstein embeddings, projecting 128 OKLab colors onto 8 Fibonacci hemisphere directions, sorting, and averaging into 16 quantiles, with L1 distance between these vectors serving as a lower bound on 1D W1. This embedding achieved 78% overlap with exact EMD&\#x27;s top 10 results and only 1.8% farther distance for the top 10, significantly outperforming other methods. For rapid nearest-neighbor queries, a custom HNSW index \(M=16, efConstruction=200\) was implemented, yielding a recall@10 of 1.000 at ef=32 and sub-millisecond search times of 0.25 ms, a substantial improvement over brute force&\#x27;s 14 ms. A modification to HNSW&\#x27;s top layer, using 8 k-means landmarks instead of random nodes, maintains search cost while making the top layer represent typical color families.

reddit · r/MachineLearning · /u/Potential-Barber8658 · Oct 11, 14:39

**「Background」** Palette Atlas is an online archive that allows users to search public-domain paintings by their color schemes, providing culturally rooted palettes rather than generic ones. When comparing color schemes, especially those reduced to a set of colors with equal weight, Earth Mover&\#x27;s Distance \(EMD\) is a natural metric because it quantifies the minimum cost to transform one color distribution into another.

**「Impact」** The Palette Atlas system demonstrates that combining Sliced Wasserstein embeddings with HNSW enables highly accurate, sub-millisecond color-scheme searches across nearly 49,000 paintings, offering a practical and efficient solution for large-scale image retrieval tasks.
