**「Impact」** This shift significantly increases the strategic importance of memory manufacturers within the rapidly evolving AI chip supply chain.

### [Article Predicts AI Tokens Will Be &\#x27;Too Cheap to Meter,&\#x27; Sparking Economic Debate](https://jyn.dev/tokens-too-cheap-to-meter/) ⭐️ 8.0/10

An article predicts that AI tokens will become &quot;too cheap to meter,&quot; suggesting that the cost of calling an LLM like GPT-5.6 Luna, currently 4-5 orders of magnitude more expensive than a \`grep\` command, will soon be even cheaper. This forecast has ignited a significant community discussion regarding the economic sustainability of AI, the potential limits of cost reduction, and the long-term viability of current business models within the AI industry. The debate centers on whether efficiency improvements can continue indefinitely and the implications for software engineering and the broader tech sector.

hackernews · teoruiz · Sep 23, 09:21 · [Discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49813482)

**「Background」** In the context of Artificial Intelligence, &quot;tokens&quot; refer to the fundamental units of text that large language models \(LLMs\) process and generate. The phrase &quot;too cheap to meter&quot; originates from a 1954 prediction about nuclear power making electricity virtually free. Applied to AI, it suggests a future where the cost of generating output tokens from AI models becomes negligible, as highlighted by an article predicting output tokens could become free, even as input tokens are priced at $42 per billion. This concept implies a significant shift in the economic model of AI usage and development.

**「Impact」** The prediction of &quot;too cheap to meter&quot; AI tokens directly challenges the economic foundations of current AI development, prompting critical re-evaluation of massive infrastructure investments and the long-term profitability of AI-centric business models.

**「Community Discussion」** Community members largely questioned the article&\#x27;s premise, with some invoking &quot;Stein&\#x27;s Law&quot; to argue that efficiency improvements cannot continue indefinitely and comparing the prediction to the unfulfilled promise of &quot;too cheap to meter&quot; nuclear power. Others criticized the article for inadequately analyzing the business model viability of AI companies, which are making substantial infrastructure investments based on expectations of future &quot;pot of gold&quot; profits.
