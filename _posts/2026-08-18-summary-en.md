### [Linux 7.3 improves performance when running out of vRAM](https://pixelcluster.dev/VRAM-Overcommit/) ⭐️ 8.0/10

The forthcoming Linux kernel update, version 7.3, is expected to significantly enhance system performance when graphics memory \(VRAM\) is fully utilized. This improvement specifically addresses the bottleneck of VRAM overcommit, which is crucial for memory-intensive applications such as AI/ML, gaming, and graphics workloads. The update aims to provide a more robust and efficient handling of VRAM resources, preventing performance degradation when these applications demand more VRAM than physically available.

hackernews · flaburgan · Aug 18, 07:51 · [Discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49342719)

**「Background」** VRAM \(Video Random Access Memory\) is dedicated memory on a graphics processing unit \(GPU\) used for storing graphical data. VRAM overcommit occurs when an application requests more VRAM than is physically available on the GPU, a scenario that GPU drivers have historically managed by deciding what data fits into the physical memory. The Linux kernel is receiving updates to improve how it handles this overcommit, particularly benefiting AMD GPUs, to prevent performance degradation when VRAM is fully utilized.

**「Community Discussion」** Community members expressed strong enthusiasm for the upcoming Linux 7.3 kernel update, anticipating significant performance gains, especially for gaming and other memory-intensive tasks. Some users highlighted ongoing challenges with VRAM management on specific hardware, such as Nvidia GPUs lacking paging support, and discussed the potential for kernel-level memory defragmentation and application-informed VRAM stickiness.

### [Field Measurements Show Data Centers Increase Neighborhood Air Temperature](https://asmedigitalcollection.asme.org/sustainablebuildings/article/7/2/024501/1233035/Data-Center-Waste-Heat-as-an-Emerging-Urban) ⭐️ 8.0/10

A peer-reviewed study presents field measurements indicating a measurable, though modest, neighborhood-scale air temperature increase attributable to data centers. The research observed a mean air temperature increase of approximately 0.8 °C, rising from 42.7 °C on the upwind side to 43.5 °C near the eastern boundary of a data center campus on the downwind side. This thermal impact extended roughly 500 meters downwind, providing concrete data to the ongoing discussion regarding the environmental footprint of critical technology infrastructure. This contribution helps inform sustainability efforts and urban planning related to software engineering, AI, and cloud computing facilities.

hackernews · cwwc · Aug 18, 17:24 · [Discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49349147)

**「Context」** Data centers consume substantial energy, a significant portion of which is dissipated as waste heat into the surrounding environment. This waste heat discharge is a known environmental concern, with previous research exploring its potential for recovery and its impact on local temperatures. A study in Phoenix, for instance, previously measured air temperature increases of up to 4 degrees Fahrenheit in downwind neighborhoods due to data center waste heat, marking the first direct measurement of such effects.

**「Impact」** Data centers contribute to a localized &quot;data heat island effect,&quot; causing measurable increases in nearby air and land surface temperatures, with studies observing rises from approximately 0.8 °C up to 4 °C, potentially influencing local microclimates.

**「Community Discussion」** Community members expressed skepticism regarding the overall alarm about data center impacts, questioning if concerns are legitimate or politically motivated, while others highlighted the specific 0.8 °C temperature delta and 500m range from the study as smaller than some headlines might suggest. There was also frustration about the difficulty of objective discussion on such topics, with some comparing the focus on data centers to less concern for other industrial polluters like oil refineries.

### [Diffusion Model Runs on 264KB RAM Microcontroller, Facing FPGA I/O Bottlenecks](https://www.reddit.com/r/MachineLearning/comments/1vrk7t5/trained_an_diffusion_model_that_runs_on_264kb_of/) ⭐️ 8.0/10

An individual successfully trained and deployed a 32x32 pixel image generation diffusion model on a Shrike Lite microcontroller, which features only 264KB of SRAM. Despite utilizing an onboard FPGA with parallel INT8 MAC engines for acceleration, the system encountered a significant memory wall due to high I/O operations, causing the FPGA-accelerated process to be slower \(220 seconds per image\) than the MCU-only execution \(70 seconds per image\). The project demonstrated the feasibility of running diffusion models on extremely resource-constrained devices, though heavy quantization and memory limits often resulted in noisy images.

reddit · r/MachineLearning · /u/PandaBean18 · Aug 18, 09:26

**「Background」** Diffusion models are a class of generative artificial intelligence models capable of producing high-quality images from noise, often used for tasks like image generation and editing. TinyML focuses on deploying machine learning models on low-power, resource-constrained embedded devices, typically requiring techniques like model quantization to reduce memory footprint and computational demands.

**「Impact」** This project highlights that even with hardware acceleration, deploying diffusion models on extremely resource-constrained microcontrollers \(264KB RAM\) can be severely limited by I/O bottlenecks, potentially leading to slower performance than non-accelerated solutions and degraded output quality due to heavy quantization.
