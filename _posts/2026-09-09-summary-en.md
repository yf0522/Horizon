### [OpenAI Claims AI Solves Navier-Stokes Millennium Problem](https://openai.com/index/navier-stokes-solution/) ⭐️ 10.0/10

OpenAI announced it has solved the Navier-Stokes existence and smoothness problem, one of the seven Millennium Prize Problems, using an internal AI system. The proof, supported by a released paper and Lean formal verification, indicates that initially smooth fluid dynamics can develop singularities in finite time. This achievement, which involved approximately 10,000 parallel agents and 130 billion output tokens, demonstrates advanced AI capabilities in complex scientific discovery, though OpenAI stated it does not intend to claim the prize. OpenAI also clarified that while they initially believed others had solved the same problem, those researchers had addressed the forced Euler equations, and OpenAI acknowledges their priority on that specific work.

**「Background」** The Navier-Stokes existence and smoothness problem is one of the seven Millennium Prize Problems designated by the Clay Mathematics Institute, each carrying a $1 million prize for its solution. This particular problem asks whether the dynamics of the Navier-Stokes equations, which describe fluid motion, can develop a singularity in finite time. A solution to any of these problems represents a significant breakthrough in mathematics.

**「Impact」** This claimed solution significantly showcases AI&\#x27;s advanced capabilities in tackling complex mathematical and scientific challenges, potentially accelerating future discoveries in fields like fluid dynamics.

**「Community Discussion」** Community discussion highlights concerns about the ethical implications of AI in research, including potential issues of credit and the impact on human-led scientific exploration, as noted by Terence Tao. Despite these concerns, many acknowledge the impressive technical feat of an AI system achieving such a complex mathematical breakthrough, with some noting the rapid capability improvement of OpenAI&\#x27;s models.

### [ASML and TSMC Advance High NA EUV with 12-inch Photomasks](https://www.nrc.nl/nieuws/2026/09/08/asml-gaat-samenwerken-met-taiwanese-chipgigant-tsmc-om-zijn-nieuwste-chipmachines-te-upgraden-a4936073) ⭐️ 9.0/10

telegram · zaihuapd · Sep 8, 06:55

**「Background」** Extreme Ultraviolet \(EUV\) lithography is a cutting-edge technology used in semiconductor manufacturing to print extremely small features on chips. High NA EUV is the next generation of this technology, offering even finer resolution by increasing the numerical aperture \(NA\) of the optical system. Photomasks are critical components in this process, acting as stencils that contain the circuit patterns to be projected onto silicon wafers.

**「Impact」** This collaboration is expected to enable more cost-effective and higher-volume production of future advanced chips for TSMC and other leading semiconductor manufacturers by addressing key productivity and cost challenges in High NA EUV lithography.

### [Qwen3.8 27B Quantization Benchmarks: 4-bit Effective, 1-bit Fails](https://quesma.com/blog/qwen38-27b-quantizations-benchmarked/) ⭐️ 8.0/10

A benchmark of Qwen3.8 27B quantizations reveals that 4-bit quantization effectively maintains performance, showing minimal degradation compared to the unquantized model. In contrast, 1-bit quantization leads to a significant collapse in performance, rendering it largely ineffective for practical deployment. These findings offer crucial insights for software engineers and AI practitioners aiming to optimize Large Language Model \(LLM\) deployment by balancing model quality with computational efficiency. The study provides practical data on performance trade-offs, highlighting the viability of 4-bit quantization for resource-constrained environments.

hackernews · stared · Sep 8, 14:49 · [Discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49611128)

**「Background」** Qwen 3.8 27B is a large language model \(LLM\) that, in its full BF16 precision, requires 55 GB of GPU RAM, making it challenging for most consumer hardware. Quantization is a technique used to reduce the size and computational demands of LLMs by lowering the precision of their weights, such as from BF16 to 4-bit or 1-bit, enabling them to run on less powerful GPUs or even CPUs. The GGUF format is commonly used for these quantized models, allowing them to operate on various hardware configurations, including CPUs with sufficient system RAM.

**「Impact」** The benchmark results provide concrete guidance for developers and AI practitioners deploying Qwen3.8 27B, indicating that 4-bit quantization offers a practical balance between performance and resource efficiency, while 1-bit quantization is generally not viable.

**「Community Discussion」** Community members debated the statistical interpretation of confidence intervals and theorized that Qwen 3.8 27B might compensate for quantization loss by increased &quot;thinking.&quot; There was also significant interest in similar benchmarks for KV cache quantizations, particularly concerning longer contexts and models fitting on GPUs with less than 16GB VRAM.

### [Argonautlabs&\#x27; Deltafin Project Runs 2.8T Kimi K3 LLM on MacBook Pro via SSD Streaming](https://github.com/argonautlabsai/deltafin) ⭐️ 8.0/10

Argonautlabs&\#x27; Deltafin project showcases a novel technique to run the 2.8-trillion-parameter Kimi K3 language model on a MacBook Pro. This is achieved by streaming model data directly from four external SSDs, effectively bypassing the system&\#x27;s limited RAM. While the current performance is extremely slow, generating only 1 token per second, this proof of concept demonstrates the technical feasibility of local inference for massive language models on consumer-grade hardware despite significant memory bottlenecks.

hackernews · Argonautlabs · Sep 8, 20:07 · [Discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49616257)

**「Background」** Kimi K3 is a 2.8-trillion-parameter Mixture-of-Experts \(MoE\) language model, activating 104 billion parameters, designed for complex tasks like coding and knowledge work with a 1-million-token context window. Models of this scale typically demand vast amounts of GPU memory, making local inference on consumer hardware like a MacBook Pro challenging due to memory constraints.

**「Impact」** This project provides a concrete demonstration that extremely large language models, previously thought impossible on consumer devices, can technically operate locally, potentially paving the way for future advancements in memory management and local AI inference.

**「Community Discussion」** Community members acknowledge the project as a significant technical &quot;start&quot; for running massive models locally, despite the current impracticality highlighted by the extremely slow performance, such as an estimated &quot;11 days&quot; for a medium prompt. Some users also drew parallels to historical computing limitations and expressed curiosity about the technical specifics of the SSD connection.
