### [Paper Questions AI&\#x27;s Translation of Navier-Stokes Proof into Formal Language](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.08144) ⭐️ 8.0/10

A new paper, arXiv:2610.08144, and subsequent community discussion are questioning the fidelity of an AI-generated formal proof of the Navier-Stokes equations. The central debate revolves around whether the AI&\#x27;s Lean proof accurately translates the original natural language mathematical arguments, rather than the correctness of the Lean proof itself. This development sparks significant discussion on the reliability and trustworthiness of AI in advanced mathematics and formal verification, particularly concerning the precise interpretation of complex mathematical concepts by large language models.

hackernews · nill0 · Oct 7, 15:24 · [Discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49994145)

**「Navier-Stokes Equations and Formal Verification」** The Navier-Stokes equations are a set of partial differential equations that describe the motion of viscous fluid substances. Proving the existence and smoothness of solutions for these equations is one of the seven Millennium Prize Problems, designated by the Clay Mathematics Institute, carrying a $1 million prize for its solution. Formal verification, often using tools like Lean, involves creating mathematically rigorous proofs in a machine-checkable format to ensure absolute correctness.

**「Impact」** This debate highlights the critical challenge of ensuring that AI-generated formal proofs accurately capture the intent and scope of their natural language counterparts, necessitating rigorous human validation of the formal theorem&\#x27;s equivalence to the original problem statement.

**「Community Discussion」** Community members highlight the paper&\#x27;s central claim that the formal Lean proof does not correspond to the natural language proof of Navier-Stokes solution blow-up, suggesting the AI may not have correctly formalized the original mathematical idea. While some question if the paper only challenges the equivalence rather than the Lean proof&\#x27;s correctness, others argue that natural language&\#x27;s inherent imprecision allows for multiple Lean translations, and the AI might have produced a minimal, valid proof. A key point of contention is whether the Lean theorem is equivalent to the problem statement published by the Clay Institute, which would determine the mismatch&\#x27;s consequence for the proof&\#x27;s overall validity.

### [God of War PSP Recompiled to WebAssembly Runs in Browser](https://github.com/snuri00/psp-web-recomp) ⭐️ 8.0/10

A project successfully recompiles the PSP game &quot;God of War&quot; from its native MIPS machine code to WebAssembly, allowing it to run directly in a web browser. This is achieved by linking the recompiled game code against a custom, small reimplementation of the PSP&\#x27;s operating system and graphics chip, which leverages WebGL2 for rendering. This novel technical approach showcases the web platform&\#x27;s advanced capabilities for executing complex, performance-critical console games without relying on traditional runtime emulation.

hackernews · sn001 · Oct 7, 11:27 · [Discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49991243)

**「Background」** The PlayStation Portable \(PSP\) was a handheld game console released by Sony in 2004, known for its graphical capabilities and a library of games that included titles like God of War. WebAssembly \(Wasm\) is a binary instruction format for a stack-based virtual machine, designed as a compilation target for high-level languages like C/C++/Rust, enabling near-native performance for web applications. WebGL2 is a JavaScript API for rendering interactive 2D and 3D graphics within any compatible web browser without the use of plug-ins.

**「Impact」** This project, which enables a copyrighted PSP game to run in a web browser, faces potential legal challenges from intellectual property holders like Sony, who typically enforce their rights against unauthorized distribution or modification of their game content.

**「Community Discussion」** Community discussion centered on whether the recompilation method still constitutes emulation, with one user noting that many emulators already use similar lift-and-JIT techniques. Other comments highlighted the graphical prowess of the PSP God of War titles and speculated on the future of server-side rendering for commercial software and potential intellectual property enforcement.
