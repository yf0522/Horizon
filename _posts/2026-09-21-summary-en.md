### [Stanford Scientists Discover Brain Composed of Two Independently Evolved Organs](https://www.solidot.org/story?sid=85426) ⭐️ 9.0/10

Stanford University School of Medicine scientists have discovered that the brain is composed of two distinct organs that evolved independently over hundreds of millions of years, challenging the centuries-old single-organ model. This finding overturns the mainstream developmental theory that a single progenitor cell gives rise to the entire brain. Researchers identified two mutually exclusive brain progenitor cell populations in developing mouse embryos: one expressing Otx2, forming the forebrain and midbrain, and another expressing Gbx2, forming the hindbrain. This suggests a more primitive brain part regulates physiological functions like heartbeat and breathing, while another confers unique human cognitive abilities such as poetry and mathematics.

**「Background」** For centuries, scientists have viewed the brain as a single, unified organ. The prevailing developmental model posited that a single progenitor cell gives rise to the entire brain, implying a common developmental origin for all its parts.

**「Impact」** This fundamental redefinition of brain structure and development could significantly reshape neuroscience research and our understanding of intelligence.

### [Pirate Face Project Uses Torrents to Preserve LLM Models](https://pirateface.co/) ⭐️ 8.0/10

The &quot;Pirate Face&quot; project aims to safeguard Large Language Models \(LLMs\) from deletion and censorship by distributing them through decentralized methods, primarily torrents. This initiative seeks to mitigate risks associated with single points of failure, such as centralized hosting platforms, ensuring the long-term availability and accessibility of these critical AI resources. The project highlights a growing concern within the AI community regarding model preservation and open-source access.

hackernews · skepticalgenius · Sep 20, 15:16 · [Discussion](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49776699)

**「Background」** Large Language Models \(LLMs\) are advanced AI models trained on vast datasets, crucial for various applications but often hosted on centralized platforms. Relying on single points of failure for distribution makes these models vulnerable to deletion, censorship, or platform outages, posing a significant threat to research and development in open-source AI. Decentralized distribution methods, like BitTorrent, offer a robust alternative by allowing users to share data directly with each other, reducing reliance on any single entity.

**「Impact」** This project directly benefits the open-source AI community by providing a resilient mechanism for preserving and accessing LLM weights, thereby reducing the risk of losing valuable models due to centralized control or policy changes.

**「Community Discussion」** Community members largely endorse using torrents for model distribution, citing their historical effectiveness for large files and their ability to circumvent single points of failure like Hugging Face. Technical discussions also emerged regarding the distribution of &quot;uncensored models,&quot; with suggestions to distribute refusal vectors rather than modified weights, and concerns were raised about the project&\#x27;s name and current lack of scripted torrent creation.
